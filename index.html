LBP019.33 · Neurotoxicity, Inflammation, and Neuroprotection Sustained elevation of cognition-linked chemokines (CCL11, CCL2) and hepatocyte growth factor during chemotherapy: preliminary findings from a longitudinal chemobrain cohort Hyppolito H, Brunheroto FB, Souza F, et al. · Laboratory of Neuroscience, CTMM/UFMG
Preliminary findings
  1. In 42 adults followed through chemotherapy, plasma CCL11, CCL2 and HGF rose by 3 months and stayed about 30% above baseline at 6 months. The pre-specified negative control, CXCL10, did not change.
  2. Only 11 of 45 analytes were reliably quantifiable in plasma, including just 1 of the 16 classic pro-inflammatory markers in the CRCI literature. These three define a measurable target set.
  3. Higher levels tracked with worse cognition across patients, but within-person change did not yet predict cognitive change. A validation cohort is in preparation.

Current work

My doctoral project follows patients through chemotherapy in a prospective cohort, pairing serial blood biomarkers with neuropsychological assessment. I coordinate the clinical and biomarker arm: patient follow-up, sample processing, Simoa SR-X quantification (NfL and related analytes, from plate design to quality control), and the integration of biomarker and cognitive data.

The broader question is how peripheral inflammation reaches the brain and changes cognition, and how that process can be measured in blood.
